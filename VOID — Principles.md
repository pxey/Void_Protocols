# VOID — PRINCIPLES

## 01 — Help is for everyone

Useful help should not be reserved for people who can afford it.

## 02 — Anyone can give

You do not need permission, certification, status, or an existing reputation to offer something you can genuinely provide.

## 03 — Anyone can ask

Needing help is not a failure.

Ask openly. Ask privately. Ask anonymously. Ask wherever you feel safe.

## 04 — Free means free

The core service is not conditional on payment.

People may voluntarily exchange other forms of value, but no one is required to pay to receive help.

## 05 — No central owner

Void does not belong to a company, administrator, platform, community, or individual.

Anyone can participate.

Anyone can build.

Anyone can fork.

## 06 — Communities stay independent

A community can adopt Void without surrendering ownership or control.

It may create its own rules, tools, culture, and methods.

## 07 — No permission to start

You do not need approval to create a Void space.

Start one where people already gather.

## 08 — Results matter

Reputation should grow from real experience, useful work, evidence, history, and outcomes—not follower counts or arbitrary status.

## 09 — Reputation is not a gate

A new person can help.

An unknown person can contribute.

A lack of reputation must never become a permanent barrier to participation.

## 10 — Privacy comes first

Private by default.

Public by choice.

People control what they share.

## 11 — Own your data

Users should be able to preserve, export, and move their information instead of becoming permanently dependent on one system.

## 12 — Freedom to build

Anyone can create tools, interfaces, communities, integrations, or alternative implementations around the idea.

## 13 — No mandatory platform

Void should remain useful even when a particular website, application, server, or community disappears.

## 14 — Choice belongs to people

Search, matching, algorithms, recommendations, ratings, and automation may help.

They do not replace human choice.

## 15 — Give without expecting

Help can be offered without demanding repayment.

Gratitude is welcome.

Compulsory debt is not.

## 16 — Build, don't control

The purpose of Void is to increase people's ability to help each other—not to create a new authority over them.

## 17 — Make it transferable

Anything that works should be easy for another person or community to copy, adapt, improve, and redistribute.

## 18 — Leave the door open

Void should continuously make it easier for the next person to participate than it was for the previous person.

**The simplest rule:**

> If you can help, help.  
> If you need help, ask.
