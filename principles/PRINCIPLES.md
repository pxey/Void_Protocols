# VOID Principles

## 01 — Help is for everyone

Useful help should not be reserved for people who can afford it.

## 02 — Anyone can give

No permission, certification, status, or existing reputation is required to offer something you can genuinely provide.

## 03 — Anyone can ask

Needing help is not a failure. Ask openly, privately, anonymously, or wherever you feel safe.

## 04 — Free means free

The core service is not conditional on payment. Voluntary forms of value exchange may exist, but no one is required to pay to receive help.

## 05 — No central owner

VOID does not belong to a company, administrator, platform, community, or individual. Anyone can participate, build, and fork.

## 06 — Communities stay independent

A community can adopt VOID without surrendering ownership or control. It may create its own rules, tools, culture, and methods.

## 07 — No permission to start

You do not need approval to create a VOID space. Start one where people already gather.

## 08 — Results matter

Useful work, evidence, history, feedback, corrections, objections, and outcomes are more meaningful than follower counts or arbitrary status.

## 09 — Reputation is not a gate

A new or unknown person can help. Lack of reputation must never become a permanent barrier to participation.

## 10 — Privacy comes first

Private by default. Public by choice. People control what they share.

## 11 — Own your data

People should be able to preserve, export, and move their information instead of becoming permanently dependent on one system.

## 12 — Freedom to build

Anyone can create tools, interfaces, communities, integrations, or alternative implementations around the idea.

## 13 — No mandatory platform

VOID should remain useful even when a particular website, application, server, or community disappears.

## 14 — Choice belongs to people

Search, matching, recommendations, ratings, and automation may help. They do not replace human choice.

## 15 — Give without expecting

Help can be offered without demanding repayment. Gratitude is welcome; compulsory debt is not.

## 16 — Build, do not control

The purpose of VOID is to increase people's ability to help one another, not to create a new authority over them.

## 17 — Make it transferable

Anything that works should be easy for another person or community to copy, adapt, improve, and redistribute.

## 18 — Leave the door open

VOID should make it easier for the next person to participate than it was for the previous person.

> If you can help, help. If you need help, ask.
