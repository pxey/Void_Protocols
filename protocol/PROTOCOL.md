# VOID Protocol v0.1

This is a **social and open protocol specification**, not a technical API specification. It defines the minimum conceptual structure required for something to be recognizable as VOID.

## Status

VOID Protocol v0.1 is an initial open specification. It may evolve through independent implementations, discussion, experimentation, and forks. No version is mandatory, and no authority certifies conformance.

## Core units

### Service

A useful thing a person or group offers freely.

### Request

A clearly expressed need for useful help.

### Connection

A voluntary relationship between people or entities that enables a service to meet a request.

**Service + Request + Connection are the core units of VOID.**

## Participation

### Give

Offer a useful service that you can genuinely provide.

### Ask

Express a need for useful help. Asking is open to everyone and does not require proof of need, identity, or reputation.

### Help

Respond to a request, provide a service, or connect people who may be able to help each other.

### Start

Create an independent VOID space, community, tool, or implementation wherever people already gather.

## Freedom

Anyone can participate. There is:

- no permission requirement;
- no mandatory account;
- no mandatory identity;
- no mandatory reputation;
- no mandatory platform;
- no mandatory payment for the core service; and
- no mandatory central authority.

## Free service principle

The core service is free. Voluntary forms of non-monetary or other value exchange may exist, but compulsory payment is not part of VOID.

## Choice

People choose providers, requesters, communities, tools, and methods. Search, algorithms, recommendations, matching, and automation are optional helpers; they do not control access or replace human choice.

## Reputation

Reputation is optional and should be grounded in evidence such as work, results, history, feedback, confirmations, corrections, and objections. Reputation is never a prerequisite for offering or requesting a service.

## Identity

No mandatory identity model exists. Anonymous, pseudonymous, and real identities can coexist. Portable identity is optional and must never become a participation gate.

## Privacy and data

Private by default. Public by choice. Data ownership and portability are core principles. People should be able to preserve, export, and move their information without permanent dependence on one VOID-controlled system.

## Communities

Communities are independent. They may establish additional rules, moderation, quality mechanisms, safety practices, tools, and culture appropriate to their context. Those local rules do not become universal VOID law.

## Interoperability

Independent communities and implementations may interoperate voluntarily. VOID does not impose one technical or organizational interoperability system.

## Forkability

Anyone may fork, modify, adapt, translate, or create an alternative implementation. No central body controls the protocol or its forks.

## Governance

There is no mandatory central authority. Changes may emerge through open discussion, implementation, experimentation, consensus, disagreement, and forks. This document is a reference, not a command structure.

## Safety and local rules

Independent communities may establish context-specific safety, legal, and risk-management rules. VOID does not create a universal centralized approval or enforcement system. Nothing in this protocol permits harmful or illegal activity.

## Conceptual conformance

A system or community can describe itself as VOID-compatible when it preserves the fundamental principles of:

- free useful service;
- open participation;
- voluntary participation;
- no mandatory central authority;
- user and community autonomy;
- freedom to fork and adapt; and
- no reputation gate.

This is a description, not a certification. No certification authority is required.
